Mathematical Architecture

How arbitrary closed & open freehand contours are integrated

1. Automatic Contour Closure (Jordan Curve Completion)

Let the user's drawn stroke be a sequence of vertices $P_0, P_1, \dots, P_m \in \mathbb{R}^2$. If $\|P_m - P_0\| > \epsilon$, the engine appends a linear closing segment $\gamma_{\text{close}}(t) = (1-t)P_m + tP_0$ for $t \in [0,1]$, guaranteeing a closed piecewise-linear Jordan contour $\partial \Omega$.

2. Vertical Scanline Riemann Partitioning (Handles Concave Loops)

Partition $[x_{\min}, x_{\max}]$ into $n$ subintervals of width $\Delta x = \frac{x_{\max} - x_{\min}}{n}$. For any sample abscissa $x_i^* \in [x_{i-1}, x_i]$, the vertical line $x = x_i^*$ intersects $\partial \Omega$ at sorted ordinates $y_1 \le y_2 \le \dots \le y_{2k}$. By the even-odd winding rule, the vertical cross-sectional length is:

$$h(x_i^*) = \sum_{j=1}^{k} \left( y_{2j}(x_i^*) - y_{2j-1}(x_i^*) \right), \quad S_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} h(x_i^*) \Delta x$$

3. Exact Limit via Green's Theorem

As $n \to \infty$, the Riemann sum converges to the double integral $\iint_\Omega dA$, which by Green's Theorem equals the boundary line integral:

$$A = \lim_{n \to \infty} \sum_{i=1}^{n} h(x_i^*) \Delta x = \frac{1}{2} \oint_{\partial \Omega} (x\,dy - y\,dx) = \frac{1}{2} \left| \sum_{k=0}^{m} (x_k y_{k+1} - x_{k+1} y_k) \right|$$
